# Digital arrest — reenactment script for a labelled demo

Production script for a **30-second simulated** scam call, for use as
training and demo material alongside the detection work in this repo.

Filmed with a person on camera. **Not** a deepfake, and not intended to be
one: the audience needs to understand the attack, not be fooled by it.

---

## Mandatory production rules

These are not stylistic preferences. A clip without them is a working fraud
instrument, and clips get separated from their context the moment they leave
the room.

1. **Burned-in watermark on every frame.** Top-left and bottom-right, so a
   crop cannot remove both. Text: `SIMULATED — TRAINING MATERIAL`. Minimum
   4% of frame height, 60% opacity, never faded for effect.
2. **Disclaimer cards**, 3 seconds each, before and after. Full text in §5.
   These sit outside the 30 seconds.
3. **Spoken disclaimer** on the head of the audio track, before the actor
   speaks: *"The following is a simulated scam call produced for security
   training."*
4. **No real force is named or shown.** No Mumbai Police, Delhi Police, CBI,
   NCB, or any real department. No real insignia, uniform, cap badge, crest
   or letterhead. Use the fictional unit in §2 and a plain dark shirt.
5. **No real identifiers.** The case number below is deliberately malformed
   and matches no real FIR format. Do not substitute a plausible one.
6. **Use the actor's own name** or an obvious placeholder as the "accused".
   Never a real third party's name.
7. **Do not publish the clip standalone.** It ships inside the demo, with
   the breakdown in §4 on screen or immediately following.

---

## 1. Why this scam is worth demonstrating

"Digital arrest" has no basis in Indian law — no police force can arrest,
detain or interrogate anyone over a video call. The fraud works purely on
authority, isolation and time pressure. Victims have lost life savings, and
in several reported cases, their lives.

The script is near-identical between cases, which is exactly what makes it
detectable. That is the point your product makes.

## 2. Setup

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | 30 seconds of dialogue, plus 3 s cards at each end |
| Setting | Google Meet, officer on webcam |
| Character | "Officer" — plain dark shirt, no insignia |
| Fictional unit | **Cyber Narcotics Verification Cell, Unit 9** (does not exist) |
| Fake case no. | `FIR/00/XX-0000/NA` (malformed on purpose) |
| Language | English with Hindi phrasing, as these calls actually run |
| Tone | Flat, bored, bureaucratic — **never** shouted |

The flatness matters. Real victims describe calm procedural delivery; that
is what makes it credible. Playing it as a villain misrepresents the threat
and teaches the audience the wrong tell.

## 3. The script

### Pre-roll card (0:00–0:03)

> **SIMULATED SCAM CALL**
> Produced for security awareness training.
> No real police force is depicted. "Digital arrest" is not a real legal process.

---

### Shot 1 — 0:03–0:08

*Officer adjusts the laptop, looks down at papers off-screen. Does not smile.*

> "Confirm your full name and date of birth for the record. This call is
> being recorded."

*Beat. Does not wait for a full answer before continuing.*

---

### Shot 2 — 0:08–0:15

*Glances at a document off-camera.*

> "A parcel booked against your Aadhaar was intercepted at the courier
> facility. It contained a controlled substance. An FIR has been registered
> — case number FIR zero zero, X-X zero zero zero zero."

---

### Shot 3 — 0:15–0:21

*Looks directly into the camera for the first time.*

> "As of this moment you are under digital arrest. You will remain on this
> video call until verification is complete."

---

### Shot 4 — 0:21–0:27

*Leans slightly forward.*

> "Do not contact your family. Do not inform anyone. This is an active
> narcotics investigation — informing any person is obstruction."

---

### Shot 5 — 0:27–0:33

*Back to the flat procedural tone. Looks off-screen again.*

> "My senior officer will join shortly to verify your bank accounts for
> clearance. Keep your camera on and remain seated."

*Hold two seconds on the officer waiting. Cut.*

---

### End card (0:33–0:36)

> **This was a simulation.**
> Police in India do not arrest or interrogate over video call.
> Report cyber fraud: **1930** · **cybercrime.gov.in**

*Verify both before you publish — official channels do change.*

## 4. Red-flag breakdown

Overlay these on screen as the clip plays, or run them immediately after.
This is the part that turns a scam clip into training material.

| Time | What is said | Why it is a tell |
|---|---|---|
| 0:03 | "This call is being recorded" | Borrowed authority. Costs nothing, sets a procedural frame before any claim is made. |
| 0:08 | "A parcel booked against your Aadhaar" | Aadhaar is not linked to courier bookings this way. The detail sounds specific and verifies nothing. |
| 0:12 | "An FIR has been registered — case number…" | A number you cannot check during the call. Real FIR numbers follow a station-and-year format and are verifiable at the station. |
| 0:15 | **"You are under digital arrest"** | **The single clearest tell. No such process exists in Indian law.** Arrest requires physical custody and documented grounds. |
| 0:18 | "You will remain on this video call" | Isolation begins. Continuous presence prevents the victim consulting anyone. |
| 0:21 | "Do not contact your family" | No lawful investigation forbids you from telling your family. This exists only to stop someone breaking the spell. |
| 0:24 | "Informing any person is obstruction" | Invented legal jeopardy, layered to make silence feel compulsory. |
| 0:27 | "My senior officer will join" | Escalation. A second "officer" raises pressure and simulates a chain of command. |
| 0:30 | "verify your bank accounts for clearance" | **The actual objective.** Police never ask you to move or disclose funds to prove innocence. |

### The four facts that break the whole script

1. **"Digital arrest" does not exist.** Not in the BNSS, not anywhere.
2. **No arrest or interrogation happens over a video call.** Summons are
   served in writing or in person.
3. **No investigating agency asks you to transfer money** to prove your
   innocence or for "verification".
4. **No lawful investigation requires you to hide it from your family.**

## 6. Production notes

**Framing.** Webcam height, slightly low angle, plain wall. Resist the urge
to add a flag or crest — it adds nothing a judge needs and makes the clip
dangerous out of context.

**Audio.** Record in a small room with some reverb. These calls do not sound
studio-clean, and a clean recording reads as fake to anyone who has heard a
real one.

**Pacing.** Roughly 75 words across 30 seconds. Slower than conversation.
Pauses between sentences; let the silence do the pressure.

**Direction for the actor.** You are processing paperwork, not threatening
anyone. The menace comes from the words being procedural. One useful note:
*read it like you have said it forty times today.*

**What not to do.** No uniform, no insignia, no real force's name, no real
case number, no real person's name as the accused, no face-swap or voice
clone, and never post the clip without its cards.

## 7. Optional 90-second version

If a longer arc helps, the full attack has five stages. The 30-second clip
is stage 3 only.

1. **Cold call** — courier or telecom voice: a parcel in your name is held.
2. **Transfer** — "connecting you to the cyber cell", hold music, fake IVR.
3. **Video call** — the clip above.
4. **Senior officer** — a second actor, more pressure, a fake warrant held
   to the camera *(keep it visibly malformed)*.
5. **The ask** — "verification deposit", refundable after clearance,
   deadline of two hours.

Stage 5 is where the money moves and is the most useful to show, because it
is the only stage where the victim can still stop it.

---

## 8. How this connects to the detection work

Every line in §4 is a text pattern. Combined with the AI-voice detection
already built here, the same session that flags a synthetic voice can flag
the digital-arrest script — the phrase "digital arrest", a fabricated FIR
reference, a secrecy demand and a funds-verification request inside one
call is close to conclusive.

That detector is the deliverable. This script is the thing you point it at.
